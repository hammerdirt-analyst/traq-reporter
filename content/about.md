Hammerdirt is the long-running work of Roger and Shannon Erismann: environmental scientists, data practitioners, and application developers
whose projects center on a simple idea — turn observations into useful information. At Hammerdirt 
that means combining fieldwork, structured data, and applied analysis in ways that support research, 
monitoring, and practical decision-making. The stated focus has evolved over more than a decade 
alongside citizen science, environmental observation, and machine learning, but the core method has 
stayed consistent: collect better observations, structure them carefully, and make the results usable.

In Switzerland, this work was built through repeated, real-world monitoring projects that demanded more 
than data collection alone. The IQAASL project for the Swiss Federal Office for the Environment required 
a maintainable end-to-end system: field methods we could operate ourselves, frontend tools adapted to 
the use case, and reporting and analysis transparent enough for both public accountability and scientific
review. The same model carried through coauthored work in academic articles and reports for public administrations.
Each project refined the same core practice: building and maintaining the data, field, and reporting 
systems needed to gather observations at scale and make the results clear, traceable, and credible.
