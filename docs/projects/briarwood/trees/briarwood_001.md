<div class="text-block" markdown="1">
The tree is assessed in the context of its suburban environment, where it is exposed to windy and rainy conditions during the winter months. The soil conditions are described as good, and there is no history of failures associated with this tree. The tree's location and the prevailing wind direction from the west contribute to its overall stability.

The primary target of this assessment is the driveway, which is located within the tree's drip line. The occupancy rate of the driveway is noted to be two, indicating that it is used by vehicles. The tree's height of 24 feet places the crown approximately 15 feet above the driveway, which is relevant for evaluating potential risks associated with falling branches.

The crown of the tree exhibits a dense structure, with a small percentage of dead twigs observed, accounting for 5% of the crown. There are no significant defects noted in the crown or branches, and the trunk is free from major concerns such as cracks or decay. The tree has been professionally pruned, which contributes to its overall health and stability.

The root collar of the tree is buried and not visible, with a depth of 2 inches. There is a 15% cavity present, but no signs of decay or dead roots have been observed. The tree's load factors indicate that it is not experiencing any recent changes that would affect its stability, and there are no soil weaknesses noted in the surrounding area.

The overall risk associated with the tree is categorized as low, with minor consequences if dead branches were to fall onto the driveway. The likelihood of failure is considered probable, but the impact is rated as low. Mitigation options include continuing regular maintenance through pruning, which is expected to maintain the residual risk at a low level.
