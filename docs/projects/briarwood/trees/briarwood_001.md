<div class="text-block" markdown="1">
The assessment of the Quercus agrifolia, a 20-inch diameter tree standing 54 feet tall, was conducted in a large urban park characterized by flat topography and pavement covering approximately 30% of the root area. The site experiences strong winds and heavy rain, but there is no history of tree failures. The prevailing wind direction is from the west, and there have been no significant changes to the soil hydrology or grade. The tree is situated in an area where its branches extend over a parking lot, which is occupied at a rate of 2 vehicles.

The Quercus agrifolia is assessed for its health and species profile, revealing a high vigor with no observed abiotic stress or pests. The foliage is entirely normal, indicating good health. The primary targets of concern are the branches that extend over the parking lot, which is within the tree's drip line. The potential impact of any branch failure could affect the vehicles parked below, with a fall distance of approximately 40 feet.

The crown of the tree exhibits dense growth, with a notable presence of dead twigs, accounting for 5% of the total crown. There are over-extended branches that pose a concern due to their proximity to the parking lot. The trunk shows signs of sap ooze from a crack on the north side, and there are codominant stems present. However, there are no significant defects such as cracks or heartwood decay noted in the trunk.

The roots and root collar are partially obscured by pavement, which covers 30% of the root area. The collar is buried and not visible, with a depth of 4 inches. There are no signs of decay or damage to the roots, and the overall condition appears stable. The main concern remains the pavement's impact on the root system, which could affect the tree's stability under load.

The overall risk associated with the tree is categorized as low. The primary risks identified include the potential for branch failure over the parking lot and the presence of sap ooze from the trunk. The likelihood of failure is considered possible but unlikely to result in significant consequences. Mitigation options include ongoing pruning and maintenance to manage the tree's health, which is expected to maintain the residual risk at a low level.
