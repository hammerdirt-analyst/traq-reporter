<div class="text-block" markdown="1">
The assessment of the Quercus agrifolia, with a diameter at breast height (DBH) of 23 inches and a height of 60 feet, was conducted in a large urban park. The site experiences strong winds and heavy rain, which are relevant factors in evaluating the tree's stability and health. There is no history of failures associated with this tree, and the soil conditions indicate that 25% of the roots are paved over. The topography is flat, and there have been no significant changes to the site that would affect the tree's health or stability.

The target assessment identified the parking lot as the primary target, which is located within the tree's drip line. The occupancy rate of the parking lot is noted to be 2, indicating a moderate level of use. Given the proximity of the tree to the parking area, any potential branch failure could impact this target.

The crown of the tree exhibits normal density with some dead twigs present, accounting for approximately 5% of the crown. There are over-extended branches that extend over the parking lot, which are the main concerns regarding potential failure. The trunk shows no significant defects, and there is no evidence of decay or damage.

The roots and root collar are affected by pavement covering 25% of the root area. While there are no visible signs of decay or damage to the roots, the presence of pavement may pose a risk to the root health and stability. No cut or damaged roots were observed during the assessment.

The overall risk associated with this tree is categorized as low. The likelihood of failure is considered possible, but the consequences of such a failure are deemed minor. Mitigation options include maintaining a regular pruning schedule to manage the over-extended branches, which is expected to result in a low residual risk after implementation.
